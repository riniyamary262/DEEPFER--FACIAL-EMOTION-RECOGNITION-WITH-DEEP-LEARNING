# DEEPFER--FACIAL-EMOTION-RECOGNITION-WITH-DEEP-LEARNING
DEEP LEARNING - INTERNSHIP MINOR PROJECT


SUMMARY

DeepFER is a deep learning-based facial emotion recognition system that automatically detects and classifies human emotions from facial expressions in images. Using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), the system categorizes facial expressions into seven primary emotions: Angry, Disgust, Fear, Happy, Neutral, Sad, and Surprise. Trained on a dataset of over 71,000 facial images, the system achieves reliable accuracy and provides emotion predictions with confidence scores. The project demonstrates the application of computer vision and deep learning for understanding human emotions, with significant potential across multiple industries. The implementation includes comprehensive data preprocessing, augmentation techniques, model training with regularization, and thorough evaluation using various performance metrics. The system is built using TensorFlow and Keras, with Python as the primary programming language.

PROBLEM STATEMENT

Facial expressions are one of the most fundamental and universal forms of human communication, yet accurately recognizing and interpreting human emotions from facial expressions remains a significant challenge in computer vision. The primary problems include variability in facial expressions across individuals based on age, gender, culture, and ethnicity, making it difficult for machines to generalize across different populations. Environmental factors such as variations in lighting conditions, different camera angles and distances, image quality issues, and occlusions like glasses, facial hair, or masks further complicate accurate detection. From a technical perspective, challenges include high computational requirements for real-time processing, the need for large and diverse datasets, class imbalance in emotion datasets, risk of overfitting due to limited or biased data, and difficulty in generalizing to unseen faces and expressions. Additionally, existing systems often lack accuracy in real-world conditions, are not robust to variations, have limited integration with practical applications, and lack accessibility for non-technical users. While significant research exists in facial emotion recognition, there remains a gap in robust models that work effectively in real-world conditions, efficient architectures that balance accuracy and computational cost, scalable solutions that can handle varying data volumes, and comprehensive systems that provide both detection and analysis capabilities.

TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE

The system utilizes a dataset of over 71,000 images with 28,000+ training images and 7,000+ test images balanced across seven emotions, all resized to 48x48 grayscale pixels sourced from the FER2013 dataset. The custom CNN architecture consists of four convolutional blocks, each with two convolutional layers followed by batch normalization, ReLU activation, max pooling, and dropout regularization. The first block has 32 filters, the second has 64 filters, the third has 128 filters, and the fourth has 256 filters, with dropout rates of 0.25 in each block. The architecture uses global average pooling instead of flattening to reduce parameters, followed by dense layers with 256 and 128 neurons with dropout of 0.5, and a final softmax output layer with 7 neurons for emotion classification. Key design features include batch normalization for accelerated training and stability, dropout regularization to prevent overfitting, global average pooling for parameter reduction, ReLU activation for better learning, and Adam optimizer with adaptive learning rate. The system also includes an alternative transfer learning model using a VGG16-style architecture. Data augmentation techniques include rotation of ±30 degrees, width and height shifts of ±20 percent, shear of ±20 percent, zoom of ±20 percent, horizontal flip, brightness adjustment between 0.8 and 1.2, and rescaling to 1/255.


IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS

The implementation uses TensorFlow and Keras for deep learning, Python 3.10+ as the programming language, PIL and OpenCV for image processing, NumPy, Pandas, and Scikit-learn for data analysis, and Matplotlib and Seaborn for visualization. The data loading process involves traversing directory structures to load images and labels, applying preprocessing and augmentation, and creating data generators for efficient batch processing. Model training utilizes early stopping with patience of 15 epochs to prevent overfitting, model checkpoint to save the best weights, and reduce learning rate on plateau with factor 0.5 and patience of 5 epochs. Class weights are computed to handle imbalance, and training runs for up to 50 epochs with a batch size of 32 and learning rate of 0.001. Prediction involves loading and preprocessing the image, passing it through the model, and returning the predicted emotion with confidence score.


RESULTS AND PERFORMANCE

The model achieves impressive performance across all seven emotion categories. For the emotion Angry, precision is 0.72, recall is 0.68, and F1-score is 0.70 with 72 percent accuracy. Disgust shows precision of 0.75, recall of 0.70, and F1-score of 0.72 with 75 percent accuracy. Fear has precision of 0.68, recall of 0.65, and F1-score of 0.66 with 68 percent accuracy. Happy demonstrates strong performance with precision of 0.79, recall of 0.82, and F1-score of 0.80 with 79 percent accuracy. Neutral shows precision of 0.73, recall of 0.70, and F1-score of 0.71 with 73 percent accuracy. Sad has precision of 0.70, recall of 0.68, and F1-score of 0.69 with 70 percent accuracy. Surprise achieves precision of 0.74, recall of 0.76, and F1-score of 0.75 with 74 percent accuracy. Overall test accuracy averages between 72 and 75 percent with validation accuracy between 70 and 73 percent, training accuracy between 85 and 90 percent, and loss between 0.45 and 0.55. Key observations include that Happy and Surprise emotions are most accurately detected, Fear and Sad show slightly lower performance, Disgust has high precision but lower recall, and Neutral is often confused with other expressions. The confusion matrix analysis reveals high confusion between Neutral and Sad, and between Fear and Surprise, while Happy shows the lowest confusion with other emotions and Fear shows the highest confusion.
